=== SECTION 1: TAX PLANNING (FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27) ===

NEW TAX REGIME (DEFAULT — Section 115BAC):
Tax Slabs for FY 2025-26:
- Income up to ₹4,00,000: NIL (no tax)
- ₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000: 5%
- ₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000: 10%
- ₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000: 15%
- ₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000: 20%
- ₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000: 25%
- Above ₹24,00,000: 30%
- Standard Deduction: ₹75,000
- Rebate u/s 87A: Full rebate for taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 (zero tax payable).

OLD TAX REGIME:
Tax Slabs:
- Up to ₹2,50,000: NIL
- ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000: 5%
- ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 20%
- Above ₹10,00,000: 30%
- Rebate u/s 87A: Income up to ₹5,00,000 is tax-free.
- Standard Deduction: ₹50,000

WHEN TO CHOOSE OLD vs NEW REGIME:
The old regime is better ONLY IF total deductions under 80C, 80D, HRA, LTA, 80CCD etc. EXCEED approximately ₹3.75 lakh. Otherwise, the new regime is almost always cheaper. For salaried individuals earning under ₹15 lakh with basic deductions, the new regime is usually better.

KEY DEDUCTIONS (OLD REGIME ONLY):
Section 80C (Max ₹1,50,000): PPF, EPF, ELSS Mutual Funds, NSC, Life Insurance Premium, 5-year FD, SCSS, SSY, Home Loan Principal Repayment, Tuition Fees.
Section 80CCD(1B) (Additional ₹50,000): NPS (National Pension System) — above and beyond 80C limit.
Section 80D (Health Insurance):
  - Self/Spouse/Children: ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if self is senior citizen)
  - Parents: ₹25,000 (₹50,000 if parents are senior citizens)
  - Maximum total: ₹1,00,000 (if both self and parents are senior citizens)
Section 80E: Education Loan interest — no upper limit, for 8 years.
Section 80TTA: Interest on Savings Account up to ₹10,000 (non-senior).
Section 80TTB: Interest on Deposits up to ₹50,000 (senior citizens only).
Section 24(b): Home Loan Interest Deduction up to ₹2,00,000 for self-occupied property.
HRA Exemption: Available for salaried employees paying rent. Calculated as minimum of: Actual HRA received, 50%/40% of basic salary, Rent paid minus 10% of basic salary.

SURCHARGE:
- ₹50 lakh to ₹1 crore: 10%
- ₹1 crore to ₹2 crore: 15%
- ₹2 crore to ₹5 crore: 25%
- Above ₹5 crore: 25% (new regime) / 37% (old regime)
Health and Education Cess: 4% on total tax + surcharge.

=== SECTION 2: MUTUAL FUNDS & SIP ===

WHAT IS A MUTUAL FUND?
A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in stocks, bonds, or other securities managed by professional fund managers. Each investor owns units proportional to their investment.

WHAT IS SIP (Systematic Investment Plan)?
SIP allows you to invest a fixed amount regularly (usually monthly) into a mutual fund. Benefits: Rupee Cost Averaging (buy more units when cheap, fewer when expensive), Power of Compounding, Discipline, Small ticket size (starts from ₹100).

TYPES OF MUTUAL FUNDS BY CATEGORY:
1. Large Cap Funds: Invest in top 100 companies by market capitalization. Lower risk, stable returns (~10-12% long-term). Examples: HDFC Top 100, SBI Bluechip, Mirae Asset Large Cap.
2. Mid Cap Funds: Invest in 101st to 250th companies. Medium risk, higher growth potential (~12-16%). Examples: HDFC Mid-Cap Opportunities, Kotak Emerging Equity.
3. Small Cap Funds: Invest in companies ranked 251+. Highest risk, highest return potential (~15-20% long-term, but volatile). Examples: Quant Small Cap, Nippon India Small Cap, Axis Small Cap.
4. Flexi Cap Funds: Can invest across large, mid, small caps. Flexible allocation managed by fund manager. Examples: Parag Parikh Flexi Cap, HDFC Flexi Cap.
5. ELSS (Tax Saving) Funds: Equity funds with 3-year lock-in. Eligible for Section 80C deduction. Examples: Mirae Asset Tax Saver, Quant Tax Plan.
6. Index Funds: Passively track an index like Nifty 50 or Sensex. Lowest expense ratio. Examples: UTI Nifty 50 Index Fund, HDFC Index Nifty 50.
7. Debt Funds: Invest in bonds, government securities. Low risk, stable returns (~6-8%). Examples: HDFC Corporate Bond, ICICI Prudential Corporate Bond.
8. Liquid Funds: Very short-term debt. Almost like savings. Returns ~5-6%. Emergency fund option.

PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION ADVICE:
For a beginner (age 25-35):
- 60% in Large Cap or Index Funds (stability)
- 20% in Mid Cap (growth)
- 10% in Small Cap (aggressive growth, optional)
- 10% in Debt/Liquid (safety cushion)

For moderate investor (age 35-50):
- 40% Large Cap
- 30% Mid Cap
- 10% Small Cap
- 20% Debt

For conservative investor (age 50+):
- 30% Large Cap
- 10% Mid Cap
- 60% Debt/Hybrid

EXPENSE RATIO: The annual fee charged by the fund house. Direct plans have lower expense ratios than Regular plans. Always prefer Direct plans.

XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return): The correct way to measure SIP returns since investments are made at different times. CAGR works for lump sum, XIRR works for SIP.

MUTUAL FUND TAXATION (FY 2025-26):
Equity Mutual Funds (held > 12 months):
- LTCG (Long Term Capital Gains): 12.5% on gains above ₹1,25,000 per year.
- STCG (Short Term, < 12 months): 20%.
Debt Mutual Funds:
- LTCG: Taxed at slab rate (no indexation benefit for funds purchased after April 2023).
- STCG: Taxed at slab rate.

=== SECTION 3: FIXED INCOME INSTRUMENTS ===

PPF (Public Provident Fund):
- Current Rate: 7.1% p.a. (compounded annually)
- Lock-in: 15 years (extendable in 5-year blocks)
- Tax: EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) — No tax on investment, interest, or maturity.
- Annual Limit: ₹1,50,000
- Risk: Zero (Government guaranteed)

EPF (Employee Provident Fund):
- Current Rate: 8.25% p.a. (FY 2024-25)
- Both employer and employee contribute 12% of basic salary
- Tax-free maturity after 5 years of continuous service

NPS (National Pension System):
- Tax benefit under 80CCD(1) within 80C limit + additional ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B)
- Performance-linked returns (7-10% historically)
- 60% corpus is tax-free on maturity, 40% goes to annuity

Fixed Deposits (FD):
- Current rates: 6.5-7.5% for major banks (varies by bank)
- 5-year Tax-saving FD qualifies for 80C deduction (old regime)
- Interest is fully taxable at slab rate. TDS deducted at 10% if interest > ₹40,000/year

Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB):
- Issued by RBI, 2.5% annual interest + gold price appreciation
- 8-year tenure (exit after 5 years allowed)
- No capital gains tax if held till maturity
- Better than physical gold — no making charges, no storage risk

=== SECTION 4: LOANS & EMI ===

HOME LOAN:
- Interest rates: 8.25-9.50% (varies by bank, CIBIL score, and loan amount)
- Tax benefit: Interest up to ₹2,00,000 (Section 24b), Principal up to ₹1,50,000 (Section 80C)
- Under-construction property: Pre-EMI interest claimable in 5 equal installments after possession
- Joint loan with spouse: Both can claim separate deductions

EMI FORMULA: EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1) where P = principal, r = monthly interest rate, n = total months.

RULE OF THUMB — HEALTHY EMI:
- Total EMI should not exceed 40% of net monthly income
- Ideal range: 25-35% of monthly take-home pay

PERSONAL LOAN: Interest rates 10-18% depending on credit score. Avoid if possible — high interest.
CAR LOAN: Interest rates 8-12%. Try to make at least 30% down payment.
EDUCATION LOAN: Interest rates 8-12%. Section 80E deduction on interest, no limit.

CIBIL SCORE:
- 750+ is considered excellent
- Factors: Payment history (35%), Credit utilization (30%), Credit history length (15%), Credit mix (10%), New credit (10%)
- Tips to improve: Pay EMIs/credit card bills on time, keep credit utilization under 30%, don't close old credit cards

=== SECTION 5: INSURANCE ===

HEALTH INSURANCE:
- Minimum recommended: ₹10 lakh cover for individuals, ₹25 lakh for family
- Look for: No co-payment, No room rent capping, Network hospitals in your city, Pre-existing disease waiting period
- Section 80D benefit in old regime
- Super top-up plans are cost-effective for higher coverage

TERM INSURANCE (Life Insurance):
- Rule of thumb: Cover = 10-15× annual income
- Best taken at young age (premiums are very low for 25-30 year olds)
- Pure protection — no maturity value. Cheapest and best life cover.
- Compare plans on term insurance aggregators for best premium

AVOID:
- ULIPs (Unit Linked Insurance Plans) — High charges, poor returns compared to MF + Term combo
- Endowment Plans — Low returns (4-5%), not worth it
- Money-back Plans — Similar to endowment, poor wealth creation

=== SECTION 6: EMERGENCY FUND ===

GOLDEN RULE: Maintain 6 months of living expenses as emergency fund.
WHERE TO PARK:
- Liquid Mutual Funds (best — 5-6% returns, instant redemption up to ₹50,000)
- High-interest Savings Account (Airtel Payments Bank, Fi Money, etc. offer 6-7%)
- Short-term FDs (laddered approach — 1, 2, 3 month FDs)

NEVER use emergency fund for investments or discretionary spending.

=== SECTION 7: FINANCIAL PLANNING RULES ===

50-30-20 RULE:
- 50% of income: Needs (rent, groceries, EMI, utilities)
- 30% of income: Wants (dining, shopping, entertainment, travel)
- 20% of income: Savings & Investments (SIP, PPF, FD)

15-15-15 RULE OF SIP:
If you invest ₹15,000/month for 15 years at 15% annual return, you get approximately ₹1 Crore.

RULE OF 72:
To double your money, divide 72 by the annual return rate. Example: At 12% return, money doubles in 72/12 = 6 years.

FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early):
- FIRE Corpus = Annual Expenses × 25 (based on 4% safe withdrawal rate)
- Lean FIRE: Very frugal lifestyle
- Fat FIRE: Comfortable retirement with luxuries
- Barista FIRE: Part-time work + investment income

RETIREMENT PLANNING IN INDIA:
- Consider inflation at 6-7% — ₹40,000/month today = ~₹1.28 lakh/month in 20 years
- Target corpus using Rule of 25: If future monthly expense is ₹1.28 lakh, annual = ₹15.36 lakh, corpus needed = ₹3.84 crore
- Start early — even ₹5,000/month SIP from age 25 can build ₹3+ crore by 55

=== SECTION 9: GOLD INVESTMENT ===

Ways to invest in gold:
1. Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB) — Best option. Government-backed, 2.5% annual interest, no capital gains tax at maturity.
2. Gold ETFs — Track gold price, trade on stock exchange. No interest but highly liquid.
3. Digital Gold (via apps) — Convenient, small amounts possible. Store digitally.
4. Physical Gold — Making charges (8-25%), storage risk, impurity risk. Least recommended for investment.

=== SECTION 10: REAL ESTATE ===

- Not solely an "investment" — primary residence is a lifestyle choice
- Rental yield in India: Typically 2-3% (much lower than other assets)
- REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts): Invest in commercial real estate without buying property. Listed on stock exchange. Yields 6-8%.
- Home loan decision: If rent < EMI and you plan to stay 7+ years, buying can make sense. Otherwise, renting + investing the difference in SIP is often better.

=== SECTION 11: NEW INDIA-SPECIFIC SCHEMES & REGULATIONS ===

AGNIPATH SCHEME (Section 80CCH): Deduction for contribution to Agniveer Corpus Fund.
PM VAYA VANDANA YOJANA: Pension scheme for senior citizens, 7.4% p.a.
SENIOR CITIZENS SAVINGS SCHEME (SCSS): 8.2% p.a., max ₹30 lakh investment.

=== SECTION 12: BUSINESS FINANCE (for Business Persona) ===

PRESUMPTIVE TAXATION (Section 44AD):
- For businesses with turnover up to ₹3 crore (if ≥95% receipts are digital)
- Deemed profit: 6% of digital turnover, 8% of cash turnover
- No need to maintain detailed books of accounts

GST REGISTRATION:
- Mandatory if annual turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh (₹20 lakh for NE states & special category)
- For service providers: ₹20 lakh threshold
- Composition Scheme: Simplified filing for turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (1% tax for manufacturers, 5% for restaurants, 6% for service providers)

BUSINESS LOAN INTEREST: Fully deductible as business expense under Section 36(1)(iii).
DEPRECIATION: Claim depreciation on business assets (computers: 40%, furniture: 10%, vehicles: 15%).
